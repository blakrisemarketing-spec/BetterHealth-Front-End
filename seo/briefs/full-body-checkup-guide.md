## H2 outline and citable opening claim per section

1. **What a full body checkup actually is** (id: `what-it-means`)
   "A full body checkup bundles several blood, urine and sometimes imaging tests into
   one visit, rather than being a single test itself."

2. **What's typically included in a full body checkup panel** (id: `whats-included`)
   "Most comprehensive checkup packages group their tests into six to eight
   categories: blood count, blood sugar, lipids, liver function, kidney function,
   thyroid, and urinalysis, sometimes with imaging or cancer markers added by age."

3. **Basic vs comprehensive: how packages differ** (id: `basic-vs-comprehensive`)
   "A basic checkup usually covers blood count, blood sugar and blood pressure, while
   a comprehensive package adds a lipid profile, liver and kidney panels, thyroid
   testing and urinalysis."

4. **Who should consider a full body checkup, and when** (id: `who-should-consider`)
   "Most healthy adults benefit from a baseline comprehensive checkup by their late
   twenties or thirties, then a repeat every one to three years depending on age,
   family history and risk."

5. **What a full body checkup does and doesn't catch** (id: `what-it-catches`)
   "A full body checkup catches many silent metabolic and organ-function problems
   early, but it is not a substitute for targeted cancer screening such as a pap
   smear, PSA test, or colonoscopy."

6. **How to choose between a basic and comprehensive panel** (id: `choosing-a-panel`)
   "Choosing between a basic and comprehensive panel comes down to your age, family
   history, symptoms and budget, not a single fixed rule."

7. **How to prepare for a full body checkup** (id: `how-to-prepare`)
   "Most comprehensive checkup packages require an 8 to 12 hour fast, because the
   bundle usually includes fasting glucose and a lipid profile."
