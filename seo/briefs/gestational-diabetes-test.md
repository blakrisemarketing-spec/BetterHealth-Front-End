## Angle

diabetes for a South African pregnant reader: what it is and why pregnancy
causes it, who gets screened early versus at the standard 24 to 28 week

## H2 outline (each opens with a self-contained citable claim)

1. **What gestational diabetes is** — Gestational diabetes is diabetes first
   diagnosed during pregnancy, caused by placental hormones making the body
   more resistant to insulin, and it usually resolves after birth.
2. **Who is screened earlier than 24 to 28 weeks** — Women with risk factors
   such as obesity, older maternal age, a family history of diabetes, a
   previous gestational diabetes diagnosis, or a previous large-for-
   gestational-age baby may be screened in the first trimester to rule out
   pre-existing diabetes.
3. **When and how screening happens (the OGTT)** — Most guidelines recommend
   screening for gestational diabetes between 24 and 28 weeks using a 75g
   oral glucose tolerance test with blood drawn fasting, then at 1 hour, then
   at 2 hours. Covers the "gestational diabetes test" secondary.
4. **The diagnostic numbers** — WHO 2013/IADPSG criteria: fasting 5.1 to 6.9
   mmol/L, OR 1-hour ≥10.0 mmol/L, OR 2-hour 8.5 to 11.0 mmol/L, any one value
   is enough to diagnose. Covers the "gestational diabetes range" secondary.
   Include a data graphic of the three thresholds.
5. **Symptoms** — Gestational diabetes usually causes no noticeable symptoms,
   which is why screening rather than waiting for symptoms is how most cases
   are found. Covers "gestational diabetes symptoms" secondary.
6. **Why HbA1c is not used to diagnose it in pregnancy** — Pregnancy changes
   red blood cell turnover, which can make HbA1c an unreliable measure of
   average blood sugar during pregnancy, so the OGTT is used instead.
7. **Diet, activity, and treatment** — Diet and physical activity changes,
   guided by a dietitian or antenatal team, control blood sugar for most
   women diagnosed with gestational diabetes. Covers "gestational diabetes
   diet" secondary. No specific meal plans or drug dosing.
8. **After birth and future risk** — Gestational diabetes typically resolves
   once the placenta is delivered, but a follow-up glucose test around 6 to
   12 weeks postpartum is recommended because it raises future type 2
   diabetes risk.
