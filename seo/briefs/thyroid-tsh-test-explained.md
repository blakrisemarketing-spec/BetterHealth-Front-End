## Angle

TSH is the test people are handed with almost no explanation, and the direction
it moves is the opposite of what most people assume: a *high* TSH usually means
an *underactive* thyroid, and a *low* TSH usually means an *overactive* one,
because TSH is the pituitary's control signal, not the thyroid hormone itself.
The article demystifies that inversion, gives the normal range, explains the
full panel (TSH, free T4, free T3, anti-TPO antibodies), and covers the West
African iodine-deficiency/goitre history that makes this test regionally
relevant (Ghana's salt iodization program).

## Citable claim per H2

1. "TSH is made by the pituitary gland, not the thyroid, and it works as a control signal that rises when thyroid hormone is low and falls when thyroid hormone is high."
2. "A TSH result outside the roughly 0.4 to 4.0 mIU/L reference range points toward an underactive thyroid when it is high, or an overactive thyroid when it is low."
3. "A single TSH reading is usually the first-line screen; free T4 and free T3 confirm the diagnosis, and thyroid antibody tests identify whether an autoimmune condition is the cause."
4. "Iodine deficiency was a major cause of goitre in West Africa before salt iodization programs, and autoimmune thyroid disease remains an under-tested cause of thyroid problems in the region today."
5. "An underactive thyroid tends to slow the body down, while an overactive thyroid tends to speed it up, and the two can look like unrelated everyday complaints."
6. Answer-first: what to ask for and when to retest.
