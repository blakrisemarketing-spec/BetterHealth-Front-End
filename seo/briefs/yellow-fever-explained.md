## Citable claim per H2
- Yellow fever is a mosquito-borne viral illness, and Nigeria sits inside the zone
  the World Health Organization classifies as having risk of yellow fever
  transmission.
- A single dose of the yellow fever vaccine gives lifelong protection, according to
  the World Health Organization's 2016 position update.
- The International Certificate of Vaccination or Prophylaxis, Nigeria's yellow
  card, is a legal requirement for international travel under the WHO International
  Health Regulations.
- Yellow fever vaccination happens at NPHCDA-approved centres and travel clinics in
  Nigeria, not as part of a routine blood test.
- Yellow fever symptoms usually start 3 to 6 days after a mosquito bite, and most
  infections stay mild.
- About 15% of yellow fever cases progress to a severe toxic phase with jaundice
  and bleeding, and this needs urgent hospital care.
- Doctors confirm yellow fever with laboratory testing, because its early symptoms
  look like malaria, typhoid, and other common fevers.
- There is no specific antiviral treatment for yellow fever, which is why
  vaccination matters more than treatment once someone is sick.
- Getting vaccinated well ahead of travel and knowing the warning signs of severe
  disease are the two most useful things to take from this article.
