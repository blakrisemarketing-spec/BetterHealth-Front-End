## Angle

Trait is not disease. The article's spine is the genetic distinction between AS
(trait, no disease) and SS/SC (disease), the inheritance odds when two carriers
have children (25% disease, 50% trait, 25% unaffected), and why West Africa's high
carrier rate exists (malaria selection pressure). Anchors to BetterHealth's own
"about 1 in 4 Ghanaians" figure already used on `/what-we-test`.

## H2 outline and citable opening claim per section

1. **What sickle cell trait actually is** (id: `what-is-sickle-cell-trait`)
   "Haemoglobin is the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen; someone with
   sickle cell trait inherited one normal copy and one sickle copy, giving the
   genotype AS."

2. **How the sickle cell test works** (id: `the-test`)
   "A sickling test gives a fast yes-or-no answer to whether any sickle haemoglobin
   is present; haemoglobin electrophoresis or HPLC identifies the exact genotype,
   distinguishing trait from disease."

3. **Why it matters before you start a family** (id: `why-it-matters-family-planning`)
   "When both partners carry the sickle cell trait, each pregnancy carries a 25%
   chance of sickle cell disease, a 50% chance the child also carries the trait,
   and a 25% chance of no sickle gene at all."

4. **Symptoms and risks for sickle cell trait carriers** (id: `symptoms-and-risks-for-carriers`)
   "Sickle cell trait is usually silent; most carriers have normal blood counts,
   normal physical performance, and a normal lifespan."

5. **Ghana and West Africa context: the malaria connection** (id: `ghana-context`)
   "Sickle cell trait is common across West Africa because it offers partial
   natural protection against severe malaria."

6. **What to do with your result** (id: `what-to-do`)
   Action-oriented ordered list: get a confirmed genotype, share with a partner,
   see a counsellor if both AS, ask during antenatal care, know your own next steps
   by result.
