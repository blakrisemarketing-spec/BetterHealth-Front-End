## Angle
Hepatitis C is the "curable" hepatitis, unlike Hepatitis B, and most people who
carry it do not know it because it is silent for years. The two-step testing logic
(antibody screen, then RNA confirmation) is the part patients misunderstand most,
plus the fact that a positive antibody test does not always mean an active,
treatable infection. Cross-link to hepatitis-b-test and liver-function-tests-explained.

## Citable claim per H2
- Hepatitis C is a blood-borne virus that infects the liver and spreads through direct blood-to-blood contact, not through casual contact, food, or water.
- The anti-HCV antibody test is the standard first screening step, and it checks whether your immune system has ever encountered the virus, not whether you currently have it.
- A positive antibody result needs a follow-up HCV RNA (PCR) test, because 15 to 25 percent of people clear the virus on their own and would test antibody-positive for life without carrying active infection.
- People with a history of blood transfusion before donor screening was standard, injection drug use, needle-stick exposure, or long-term dialysis carry the highest risk and should prioritise testing.
- Direct-acting antiviral treatment now cures more than 95 percent of hepatitis C infections within 8 to 12 weeks, a shift from the older, harder interferon-based treatment era.
