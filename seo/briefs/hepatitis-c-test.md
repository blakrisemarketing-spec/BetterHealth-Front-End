## Citable claim per H2
- The hepatitis C test looks for antibodies to the hepatitis C virus in your blood, and a positive antibody result is followed by an RNA test to confirm whether the virus is still active.
- Most people with hepatitis C have no symptoms for years, so the infection is usually found through screening or an incidental blood test, not because someone feels unwell.
- A positive anti-HCV antibody test means past exposure, but only a positive HCV RNA test confirms the virus is still active in your body right now.
- People who have ever received unscreened blood or blood products, shared needles or injecting equipment, or had procedures with poorly sterilised equipment face a higher risk and should ask about screening.
- Hepatitis C has no vaccine, but unlike hepatitis B it is curable in the large majority of people with a short course of modern antiviral tablets.
- A positive RNA result needs a treatment plan from a doctor, not panic, since cure rates with current antivirals are high.
