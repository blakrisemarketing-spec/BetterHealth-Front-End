## H2 outline (with the citable opening claim for each)

1. **Why routine screening matters in Nigeria** — Several of the conditions responsible for the most illness and early death in Nigeria, including high blood pressure, diabetes, sickle cell disease and hepatitis B, produce few or no symptoms until they have already caused damage.
2. **Nigeria's disease burden shapes what to test** — Nigeria has the highest number of sickle cell disease births of any country in the world, alongside a heavy burden of hypertension, diabetes, malaria and hepatitis B, and the screening panel that makes sense here reflects that mix.
3. **The core screening panel for adults** — A sensible baseline panel for most Nigerian adults covers blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol, full blood count, genotype and sickle cell status, hepatitis B, and HIV, with a few tests added by sex and age.
4. **Extra tests by sex and age** — Women benefit from regular cervical screening from their mid-20s, and men from around 40 should discuss a PSA test with a doctor if they have symptoms or a family history of prostate cancer.
5. **How often to screen** — Healthy adults in their 20s and 30s can screen every two to three years, adults 40 and older benefit from a fuller check every one to two years, and anyone with a family history of hypertension, diabetes or sickle cell disease should screen earlier and more often.
6. **Where and how screening happens in Nigeria** — Screening is available through government and teaching hospitals, private hospitals and diagnostic labs, employer or NHIS/HMO-linked checkups, and a growing number of home-collection services, each with different cost and convenience trade-offs.
