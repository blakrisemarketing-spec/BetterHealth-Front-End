## Angle
Most women searching "perimenopause test" want a single number that confirms what is
happening to them. The honest, patients-led answer is that FSH is genuinely useful but
noisy during perimenopause itself, so guidelines lean on symptoms and age first and
reserve hormone testing for younger women or unclear cases. Cover the definitions
(perimenopause vs menopause), the symptom list, what FSH/estradiol actually show, why

overlap as a genuine differential, and where treatment conversations (HRT, non-hormonal
options) start. Keep the "one FSH reading is not the final word" nuance intact through

## H2 outline (each opens with a self-contained, citable claim)
1. Menopause is defined as 12 months without a period, and perimenopause is the years-long transition leading up to it
2. Irregular periods, hot flushes, night sweats, sleep and mood changes are the symptoms that usually point to perimenopause first
3. FSH rises and estradiol falls as ovarian function declines, which is what a hormone test is looking for
4. A single FSH test during perimenopause can read normal one day and high the next, so it is not reliable on its own
5. Over-the-counter FSH test kits measure the same hormone as a lab test and carry the same one-reading limitation
6. Major guidelines reserve routine hormone testing for women under 45 or unclear cases, not typical perimenopause over 45
7. Fatigue, mood change and irregular cycles can also signal a thyroid problem, which is why differentials matter
8. Hormone replacement therapy and non-hormonal options exist, and the right choice is a conversation with a doctor

## Citable claim per H2
- Menopause is confirmed clinically after 12 consecutive months without a period, typically between ages 45 and 55, and perimenopause is the transition that can last several years before that.
- Irregular periods, hot flushes, night sweats, sleep disturbance, mood changes, vaginal dryness and reduced fertility are the symptom cluster clinicians look for first in women of the typical age.
- FSH rises as the ovaries produce less estrogen, and a high FSH alongside a low estradiol supports a diagnosis of menopause.
- Hormone levels fluctuate significantly during perimenopause, so one FSH result can be normal one day and elevated the next, which is why a single test cannot diagnose perimenopause on its own.
- Over-the-counter urine-based FSH test kits sold at pharmacies measure the same hormone as a lab blood test and carry the same single-reading limitation.
- NAMS (The Menopause Society) and NICE generally advise against routine hormone testing in women over 45 with typical symptoms, and reserve it for women under 40 to 45 or cases where the picture is unclear.
- Perimenopause symptoms such as fatigue, mood change and irregular cycles overlap with thyroid problems, which is a reason clinicians check both rather than assume one cause.
- Hormone replacement therapy works well for many women but carries considerations tied to personal and family health history, which is why it is decided with a doctor.
