## Why this article
Low direct search volume on the exact phrase but it anchors a real cluster (b12 deficiency,
b12 foods, b12 tablets/injection, dosage) and completes the anaemia work-up story alongside
the ferritin article. Iron deficiency (microcytic) and B12/folate deficiency (macrocytic) are
the two anaemia stories patients search for; without this piece the ferritin article has no
partner for "why is my MCV high" style queries.

## Angle
Patients-led, answer-first. Most people only hear about B12 when a doctor mentions it after
an FBC comes back with a high MCV, or when fatigue and tingling hands send them looking for
answers. Frame B12 and folate together (both cause the same macrocytic anaemia picture) but be
clear they are different nutrients with different causes and different fixes. Give real
non-alarmist urgency to the neurological symptoms point: it is genuinely worth acting on
promptly, but it is detectable and treatable.

## H2 outline (each opens with a self-contained, citable claim sentence)
1. **What a vitamin B12 test measures** — a B12 test measures how much vitamin B12 is
   circulating in your blood, the nutrient your nerves and red blood cells depend on.
2. **Symptoms of B12 deficiency** — B12 deficiency causes fatigue and pale skin at first, then
   nerve symptoms like tingling hands and feet, poor balance, and memory trouble as it
   progresses.
3. **What causes vitamin B12 deficiency** — B12 deficiency comes from eating too little of it,
   absorbing too little of it, or both, and the balance between those two causes varies widely
   across African diets and health conditions.
4. **Folate and megaloblastic anaemia** — folate deficiency produces the same enlarged,
   under-formed red blood cells as B12 deficiency, so a folate test is usually ordered
   alongside a B12 test, not instead of it.
5. **B12/folate anaemia vs iron-deficiency anaemia** — B12 and folate deficiency shrink your
   red cell count but make each remaining cell abnormally large, the opposite pattern to the
   small cells of iron deficiency, and an FBC's MCV is what tells them apart.
6. **Vitamin B12 foods** — vitamin B12 occurs naturally only in animal-source foods, which is
   why diets built mainly around plant staples need a deliberate plan to cover it.
7. **Treatment: B12 tablets and B12 injections** — low B12 is treated with oral tablets or
   injections depending on the cause and how low the level is, and a doctor decides which one
   and for how long.
8. **What to do with your result** — a low B12 or folate result is a starting point for your
   doctor to find the cause, not a diagnosis to self-treat.
