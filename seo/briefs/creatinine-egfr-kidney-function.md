## Angle
Creatinine and eGFR are usually ordered together but reported very differently: one is a raw lab number, the other is a calculated percentage of kidney function. Most patients have never had either explained. The hook: high blood pressure and diabetes, both common in Ghana, are the two biggest causes of kidney damage, and a kidney function test is often the first place that damage shows up before symptoms do.

## H2 outline (each opens with a self-contained citable claim)

1. **What creatinine and eGFR measure** — Creatinine is a waste product from muscle activity that healthy kidneys filter out of the blood; eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate) uses your creatinine, age, and sex to estimate how well your kidneys are filtering.
2. **Creatinine normal range** — Typical creatinine reference ranges are about 60 to 110 micromol/L for men and 45 to 90 micromol/L for women, though the exact cutoff varies by lab and by muscle mass.
3. **eGFR ranges and what they mean** — eGFR is reported in stages, from normal (90 or above) down to kidney failure (below 15), and most labs flag anything under 60 for follow-up.
4. **Why creatinine and eGFR move in opposite directions** — As kidney function declines, creatinine builds up in the blood while the calculated eGFR falls, so a rising creatinine and a falling eGFR describe the same trend from two directions.
5. **Why hypertension and diabetes make this test matter in Ghana** — High blood pressure and diabetes are the two leading causes of chronic kidney disease in Ghana, and kidney damage from either condition often has no symptoms until function has already dropped significantly.
6. **Symptoms of reduced kidney function** — Early kidney function loss usually causes no symptoms at all, which is why the blood test, not how a person feels, is what catches it.
