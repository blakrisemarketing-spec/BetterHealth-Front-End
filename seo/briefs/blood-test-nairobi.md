Nairobi-specific, not a Ghana article with the city name swapped. Cover the practical

## H2 outline (each opens with a self-contained citable claim)

1. **What people mean by "a blood test in Nairobi"** — A blood test in Nairobi can
   mean anything from a single walk-in check to a full body checkup panel of a dozen
   or more markers, and where you go depends on which one you need.
2. **Where to get a blood test in Nairobi** — Nairobi residents can draw blood at
   public hospitals, private hospitals, standalone diagnostic labs, and home draw
   services, each with a different trade-off of cost, wait time, and convenience.
3. **What a full body checkup in Nairobi usually covers** — A full body checkup in
   Nairobi typically bundles blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol, kidney and
   liver function, a full blood count, and screening for locally common infections
   like malaria, typhoid, HIV, and hepatitis B into one visit. (covers secondary kw)
4. **Walk-in lab vs. home blood draw vs. hospital lab** — comparison graphic; each
   option suits a different kind of Nairobi schedule and budget.
5. **Fasting and preparing for a blood test in Nairobi** — Several of the most common
   tests in a Nairobi panel need 8 to 12 hours of fasting beforehand, so what you eat
   the night before matters more than the clinic you pick.
6. **Paying for a blood test in Nairobi: NHIF/SHA and out-of-pocket** — Kenya's
   national health cover has been shifting from NHIF to the Social Health Authority
   (SHA), and what a blood test costs out of pocket still varies by facility and
   panel size. (hedged, general, no invented figures)
7. **How long results take** — Turnaround for a Nairobi blood test ranges from same
   day for common tests to several days for specialised panels, and the lab should
   tell you the expected time when you book.
8. **Choosing where to test in Nairobi** — A short checklist for picking a facility.

## Clinical / local sourcing notes

- NHIF-to-SHA transition: Kenya has been moving its national health insurance
  framework from the National Hospital Insurance Fund (NHIF) to the Social Health
  Authority (SHA) under the Social Health Insurance Act. Stated only in general
  terms here (no specific coverage amounts or dates claimed) since exact current
  status was not independently verified at draft time.
- Regulatory framing: mentioned only in general terms (statutory registration of lab
  professionals in Kenya) without naming a specific pinpoint citation, per the
  "hedge rather than invent" instruction.
- Named facility: Kenyatta National Hospital (KNH), Kenya's flagship public
  referral hospital, mentioned once as a widely known example of the public-hospital
  option. No other named labs/facilities used, and no prices are stated as fact.
- Disease burden references (malaria, typhoid, HIV, hepatitis B as common reasons
  Nairobi panels include infectious-disease screening) kept general, consistent with
  the disease framing already used in `malaria-test-explained.js` and
  `hiv-test-explained.js` on this site.
