## H2 outline and citable opening claim per section

1. **What an allergy test checks for** (id: `what-an-allergy-test-checks-for`)
   "An allergy test checks whether your immune system has built antibodies against a specific substance, not whether that substance will definitely cause a reaction."

2. **Allergy symptoms** (id: `allergy-symptoms`)
   "Recurring hives, swelling, digestive upset after specific foods, or breathing symptoms tied to a clear trigger are the signs that usually prompt allergy testing in the first place."

3. **Skin prick test** (id: `skin-prick-test`)
   "A skin prick test introduces a tiny amount of allergen just under the skin and reads the reaction after about fifteen to twenty minutes."

4. **Specific IgE blood test** (id: `specific-ige-blood-test`)
   "A specific IgE blood test measures allergen-targeted antibodies in a blood sample and is the usual choice when skin testing is not practical."

5. **Skin prick vs blood: which one** (id: `skin-prick-vs-blood-which-one`)
   "Choosing between a skin prick test and a blood allergy test usually comes down to practicality rather than accuracy, since both are useful, established tools."

6. **Food allergy test panels** (id: `food-allergy-test-panels`)
   "A food allergy test panel typically screens a shortlist of common triggers rather than testing every food at once, because broad, unfocused panels tend to produce more false positives than useful answers."

7. **What a positive result means** (id: `what-a-positive-result-means`)
   "A positive allergy test result means sensitization has been detected, which is not automatically the same as having a clinically significant allergy."

8. **Eosinophils and the FBC** (id: `eosinophils-and-fbc`)
   "A routine full blood count can show a raised eosinophil count as an incidental clue toward an allergic or parasitic process, but this is not the same as a diagnostic allergy test."

9. **South Africa context** (id: `south-africa-context`)
