## Intent

## Angle
No sourced Ghana cedi pricing data exists for this draft, and BetterHealth's own
prices change independently of this article, so stating specific figures here would
be a false, quickly-stale claim. The differentiated angle is cost **literacy**: teach
the reader the handful of variables that actually move the price of a health check
(bundle vs single test, public vs private, lab vs home draw, panel size) so they can
evaluate *any* quote intelligently, then send them to the live `/pricing` page for
real numbers. This is a defensible, citable answer for AI engines even without a
hard price point, and it positions BetterHealth as the transparent option rather than
the one hiding behind a vague "starting from" figure.

## H2 outline (each opens with a self-contained, citable claim)

1. **What "a full health check" usually means in Ghana** — A full health check in
   Ghana usually means a bundled panel of blood tests grouped by body system and
   ordered together in a single visit, rather than one test booked at a time.
2. **Basic vs comprehensive: what's typically included** — A basic screening panel
   typically covers blood pressure, blood sugar and a full blood count, while a
   comprehensive panel adds kidney, liver, lipid and, sometimes, hormone markers on
   top of that base.
3. **What actually drives the price of a health check** — Four variables mostly
   decide what a health check costs: whether you buy a bundle or single tests,
   whether the provider is public or private, whether the sample is drawn at home or
   in a lab, and how many biomarkers the panel covers.
4. **Bundles vs single tests: why panels usually cost less per test** — Buying a
   bundled panel is almost always cheaper per test than ordering the same tests one
   at a time, because bundling spreads a lab's fixed processing costs across more
   results.
5. **Public vs private, and lab vs home draw** — Public and government-linked
   facilities are generally the lower-cost route for routine tests in Ghana, while
   private clinics and home-collection services price in convenience, speed and
   staff time.
6. **How to judge value, not just the lowest price** — The cheapest option is not
   automatically the best one; what should decide it is whether the lab is
   accredited, the result is delivered on time, and someone qualified explains it to
   you.
7. **Does NHIS or private insurance cover health screening?** — Ghana's National
   Health Insurance Scheme covers a limited set of diagnostic tests tied to a
   diagnosed illness or condition, and it does not generally cover routine
   preventive screening chosen by someone who feels well.
8. **How to actually budget for a health check** — The most reliable way to budget
   for a health check in Ghana is to decide which panel fits your age and risk
   first, then compare current, live pricing across a couple of providers, since
   list prices move and a printed figure goes stale fast.
