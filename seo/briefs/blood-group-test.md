## Citable claim per H2
- A blood group test measures two separate things in one draw: your ABO type (A, B, AB, or O) and your Rh factor (positive or negative).
- Combining ABO type and Rh factor gives eight possible results, from O negative to AB positive, and each carries different rules for who you can safely receive blood from.
- O negative is called the universal red-cell donor type because it can be given to patients of any blood type in an emergency, while AB positive is the universal recipient for red cells.
- An Rh-negative mother carrying an Rh-positive baby needs monitoring and, when indicated, an anti-D injection during pregnancy to prevent Rh sensitisation, a preventable complication that once caused serious harm in later pregnancies.
- Rh-negative blood is notably rarer in African populations, generally under 5 percent, than in European-descended populations, which makes matched blood for Rh-negative patients harder to source locally and raises the value of knowing your own type in advance.
