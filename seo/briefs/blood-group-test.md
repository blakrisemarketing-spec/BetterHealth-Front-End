## H2 outline and citable claim per section

1. **What your blood group actually is (the ABO system)**: Every
   person's blood falls into one of four ABO groups, A, B, AB or O, based on
   which sugar markers sit on the surface of their red blood cells.
2. **How your ABO group is determined**: Your ABO blood group is set
   by genes inherited from both parents and does not change during your
   lifetime.
3. **The Rh factor: positive or negative**: The Rh factor is a
   separate marker, the D antigen, that is either present (Rh-positive) or
   absent (Rh-negative) on your red blood cells, and it is reported
   alongside your ABO group as the "+" or "-" in a result like O+.
4. **Why blood type matters for transfusion**: Mixing incompatible
   blood types in a transfusion causes the immune system to attack the donor
   red cells, which is why every unit of donated blood is matched to a
   recipient's ABO and Rh type before use.
5. **Universal donor and universal recipient**: O-negative blood
   carries no A, B or Rh(D) markers for a recipient's immune system to react
   against, which is why it is called the universal donor type, while
   AB-positive people can receive any ABO/Rh combination and are called
   universal recipients.
6. **Rh factor and pregnancy**: An Rh-negative mother carrying an
   Rh-positive baby can develop antibodies against the baby's blood type,
   a situation called Rh incompatibility, which is why Rh status is checked
   at the first antenatal blood test.
7. **Blood donation in an African health-system context**: Many
   African blood banks, including Ghana's, report chronic shortages against
   demand, and patients with sickle cell disease are among the most frequent
   recipients of transfusion because their condition can require repeated
   transfusions over a lifetime.
8. **How to find out your blood group**: A blood group and Rh test
   is a simple blood draw that most labs can process the same day, and it is
   commonly done before surgery, during antenatal care, or as part of
   premarital and pre-donation screening.
