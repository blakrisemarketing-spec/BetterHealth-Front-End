## H2 outline (each opens with a standalone, citable claim)

1. What a genotype test measures — identifies the haemoglobin genes inherited from each parent, reported as a
   two-letter code (AA, AS, SS, SC, AC, CC).
2. How the test is done — a small blood sample, analysed by haemoglobin electrophoresis or HPLC; no fasting;
   caution against relying on informal "genotype test kits" or card tests for a marriage decision.
3. Genotype compatibility for couples — what each pairing (AA+AA, AA+AS, AA+SS, AS+AS, AS+SS, SS+SS) means for
   future children's risk of sickle cell disease.
4. Why this matters in Nigeria — Nigeria has the highest number of children born with sickle cell disease of
   any country; roughly 1 in 4 people carry the sickle trait, similar to the wider West African rate.
5. What to do with your result — get a lab-confirmed genotype, share and discuss with a partner, see a genetic
   counsellor if a pairing carries risk, options if you're already expecting.
