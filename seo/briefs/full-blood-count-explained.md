## H2 outline and citable opening claim per section

1. **What the FBC tests** (id: `what-fbc-tests`)
   "A full blood count (FBC) is a single blood test that measures three main cell types: red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets."

2. **Haemoglobin and red cells: your oxygen-carrying capacity** (id: `haemoglobin-red-cells`)
   "The WHO defines anaemia as haemoglobin below 130 g/L in men and below 120 g/L in women."

3. **MCV: what the size of your red cells tells you** (id: `mcv-red-cell-size`)
   "MCV below 80 fL (microcytic) most often points to iron deficiency; MCV above 100 fL (macrocytic) suggests B12 or folate deficiency."

4. **White blood cells: infection and immunity** (id: `white-cells`)
   "A WBC count above 11.0 ×10⁹/L (leukocytosis) usually signals infection or inflammation; below 4.0 ×10⁹/L (leukopenia) can indicate a viral illness or bone marrow problem."

5. **Platelets: clotting and bleeding risk** (id: `platelets`)
   "A platelet count below 150 ×10⁹/L (thrombocytopenia) can cause bruising and slow wound healing; above 400 ×10⁹/L (thrombocytosis) may indicate inflammation or iron deficiency."

6. **Ghana-specific context: sickle cell, malaria, and iron deficiency** (id: `ghana-context`)
   "About one in four Ghanaians carries sickle cell trait (HbAS), which shortens red cell lifespan and makes haemoglobin and MCV readings harder to interpret without knowing the patient's haemoglobin genotype."
