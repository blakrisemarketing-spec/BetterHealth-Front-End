## Angle
The "you can't be deficient in a sunny country" myth is persistent and medically wrong.
Studies across sub-Saharan Africa show 30-60% of adults are insufficient or deficient.
This article busts the myth, explains the test, gives the ranges, and tells people what to do.

## One-sentence citable claim per H2
1. "The standard vitamin D test measures 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25(OH)D), the form produced by the liver after absorption from sunlight, food, or supplements."
2. "A 25(OH)D result of 50 nmol/L or above is considered sufficient; 30 to 49 nmol/L is insufficient; below 30 nmol/L is deficient, per Endocrine Society guidelines."
3. "Despite abundant sunshine, studies across sub-Saharan Africa find 30 to 60 percent of adults have insufficient or deficient vitamin D, driven by melanin reducing UV-B absorption efficiency, indoor work, and low dietary intake."
4. "Vitamin D deficiency often causes no symptoms until severe; when it does, symptoms include bone pain, muscle weakness, fatigue, frequent infections, and in children, rickets."
