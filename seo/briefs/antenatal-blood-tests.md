## Citable one-sentence claims (GEO-ready, answer-first for each H2)

- Antenatal blood tests exist to catch conditions that have no symptoms early enough in pregnancy to act on them, for both mother and baby.
- A blood group and Rhesus factor test at the first antenatal visit flags Rh-negative mothers who may need extra monitoring during pregnancy.
- A full blood count at booking screens for anaemia, one of the most common and most treatable conditions in pregnancy worldwide.
- HIV and hepatitis B screening at the first antenatal visit lets treatment or prevention of mother-to-child transmission start as early as possible.
- Genotype or sickle cell screening in pregnancy matters most in West and Central Africa, where the sickle cell trait is carried by a large share of the population.
- Gestational diabetes screening is usually done around 24 to 28 weeks of pregnancy, later than most of the other booking bloods.
- Most of the core antenatal panel is drawn once at the first booking visit, though a few tests, including full blood count and blood sugar, are commonly repeated later in pregnancy.
