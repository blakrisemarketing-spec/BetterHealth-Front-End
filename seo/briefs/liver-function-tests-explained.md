## Citable claim per H2
- ALT and AST are enzymes that leak out of damaged liver cells into the blood, so both usually rise together when the liver is under strain.
- Typical reference ranges run about 7 to 56 U/L for ALT and 10 to 40 U/L for AST, though exact cutoffs vary by lab.
- ALT is more liver-specific than AST, because AST is also found in muscle and heart tissue.
- An AST:ALT ratio above 2 can suggest alcohol-related liver damage, though the ratio alone never confirms a diagnosis.
- GGT is often added to the panel because it rises with alcohol use and bile duct problems that ALT and AST can miss.
- Hepatitis B and fatty liver disease are two of the most common causes of raised liver enzymes across sub-Saharan Africa.
