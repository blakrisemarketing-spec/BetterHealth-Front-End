## Angle
The broad overview article for the batch. Covers the three-pronged approach to breast
cancer awareness and screening: breast self-awareness/self-exam (what's normal vs. what
changes warrant a doctor visit), clinical breast exam by a provider, and mammography
covered only at a summary level (age/risk-group guidance, no procedure mechanics). Risk
factors covered with the deliberate hedge that most people who develop breast cancer have
no strong family history, so its absence should not create false reassurance. No
fear-mongering language; early detection framed as improving outcomes, not as urgency
messaging. Pan-African framing: breast cancer is one of the most common cancers among
African women and is often detected at a later, harder-to-treat stage across the
continent due to lower screening coverage, sourced qualitatively to WHO/global cancer
registries, no invented precise statistic.

## H2 outline (each opens with a self-contained claim)
1. `three-approaches` — Breast cancer screening rests on three approaches, not one test.
2. `self-awareness` — Breast self-awareness means knowing what is normal for you, not
   hunting for problems (includes `changes-to-watch-for` h3 + list of red-flag changes:
   new lump, skin dimpling, nipple changes, unusual discharge).
3. `clinical-breast-exam` — A clinical breast exam adds a trained hand to what you
   already notice.
4. `mammograms-briefly` — A mammogram is a low-dose breast X-ray recommended from a
   certain age or risk level, and it deserves its own deep dive (kept to 2 short
   paragraphs, hands off to the batch-mate article for procedure detail).
5. `risk-factors` — Age and family history raise risk, but most people who develop
   breast cancer have neither.
6. `why-africa` — Breast cancer is often caught later across Africa, and that gap is
   closing unevenly.
7. `what-to-do` — Put the three approaches into a routine you can actually keep
   (ordered action list).
