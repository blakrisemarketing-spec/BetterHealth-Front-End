## Search intent

Informational: a patient has received a ferritin or iron panel result, or suspects iron deficiency, and wants to understand what the numbers mean and what to do next.

## Angle

Ferritin is the earliest and most sensitive marker of iron deficiency — it falls before haemoglobin drops. Explain the three-stage model (depleted stores, functional deficiency, anaemia), the full iron panel, and the Ghana-specific context where malaria, fibroids, and sickle cell disease make the picture more complex.

## H2 outline and citable opening claim per section

1. **What ferritin measures** (id: `what-ferritin-measures`)
   "Ferritin is the protein your body uses to store iron inside cells; a ferritin blood test measures how much iron you have in reserve, not just what is circulating right now."

2. **Normal ferritin levels: the reference ranges** (id: `normal-ferritin-levels`)
   "Standard clinical reference intervals are 30 to 300 µg/L in men and 12 to 150 µg/L in women; a ferritin below 12 µg/L is consistently classified as deficient across all major guidelines."

3. **Low ferritin: iron deficiency before the anaemia** (id: `low-ferritin`)
   "Iron deficiency develops in stages — ferritin falls first, often while haemoglobin still reads normal, making it the earliest detectable sign of iron depletion."

4. **Symptoms of iron deficiency anaemia** (id: `iron-deficiency-symptoms`)
   "Iron deficiency anaemia produces fatigue, pallor, breathlessness, brittle nails, hair loss, and in some people pica — cravings to eat ice, chalk, or clay."

5. **High ferritin: what raised storage iron can signal** (id: `high-ferritin`)
   "A raised ferritin does not always mean iron overload; ferritin is also an acute-phase reactant and rises with infection, inflammation, and liver disease."

6. **The full iron panel: tests doctors order together** (id: `iron-panel`)
   "The ferritin test is most informative alongside serum iron, total iron binding capacity (TIBC), and transferrin saturation, which together distinguish iron deficiency from anaemia of chronic disease."

7. **Ghana context: why iron deficiency anaemia is common** (id: `ghana-context`)
   "Iron deficiency anaemia disproportionately affects women of reproductive age, children under five, and pregnant women in Ghana, driven by malaria, heavy menstrual bleeding (including from fibroids), and dietary patterns low in haem iron."

## Ranges source

Standard clinical reference intervals widely adopted in clinical guidelines (WHO, British Society for Haematology). Ferritin < 12 µg/L = deficient per WHO. Men 30–300 µg/L, Women 12–150 µg/L per standard clinical practice. Serum iron 10.7–26.9 µmol/L; transferrin saturation 20–45%.
