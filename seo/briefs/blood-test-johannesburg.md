## Angle

First South-Africa-market article on the site. Uses the same three-route
framework established for Accra/Lagos/Nairobi/Kumasi (public hospital, private
pathology lab, home sample collection), retargeted with South-Africa-specific
funding and disease-burden context rather than reused Kenya/Nigeria content.
The differentiating angle:

1. South Africa's funding model is explicitly different from Ghana/Nigeria/Kenya:
   most private testing runs through medical aid schemes (private health
   insurance) or direct out-of-pocket payment, alongside a public health system
   that serves patients regardless of medical aid status. Medical aid, gap
   cover, co-payments, and NHI (National Health Insurance) reform are all kept
   general and hedged, no coverage amounts, no scheme names' benefits stated,
   and NHI is described as an ongoing, evolving reform, never as already fully
   implemented.
2. Chris Hani Baragwanath Academic Hospital named once as the widely known
   public-hospital example (one of the largest hospitals in Africa, located in
   Soweto), used illustratively only, not as a specific-services claim.
3. Private pathology labs described generically ("private pathology labs,"
   "diagnostic centres") given South Africa's well-established private
   pathology sector, no specific brand named as an endorsement.
4. Johannesburg framed as a major commercial hub with heavy traffic, the
   genuine, non-hard-sell reason home sample collection is worth naming as a
   category (travel time across the metro, not a sales pitch).
5. South Africa's high HIV and TB burden, alongside rising hypertension,
   diabetes, and other non-communicable diseases, named honestly and
   respectfully in qualitative terms only, consistent with the disease-burden
   framing already used in hiv-test-explained.js and malaria-test-explained.js
   on this site. No invented statistics.
