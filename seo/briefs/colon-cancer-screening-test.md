## Search intent

Informational. Someone has either noticed a symptom (blood in stool, a change in bowel habits), been told by a doctor to get screened, or is trying to decide between a stool test and a colonoscopy and wants to understand both options honestly before choosing.

## Angle

Balanced, non-alarmist explanation of the two real colon cancer screening routes: stool-based tests (FIT/FOBT occult blood, and stool DNA tests) versus colonoscopy. Both are framed as legitimate, guideline-recognised options with real tradeoffs, not one being "better" in every case: stool tests are non-invasive and repeatable but need repeating and can miss non-bleeding polyps; colonoscopy is more sensitive and doubles as treatment (polyp removal) but is invasive, needs a full bowel prep, sedation, and specialist/facility access that is not evenly distributed. Colorectal cancer symptoms (bowel habit change, blood in stool, unexplained weight loss, persistent abdominal pain) are covered with a clear hedge that these are far more often caused by benign conditions such as haemorrhoids or IBS, so they are a reason to see a doctor, not to panic or to self-diagnose. Age guidance follows the same hedged pattern used in the Pap smear article: USPSTF's 2021 move to age 45 for average-risk adults is cited as the mainstream anchor, with an explicit note that South Africa has no single official national colorectal screening age and that family history or symptoms should move the conversation earlier. South African framing uses medical scheme terminology (benefit, pre-authorisation, co-payment), not NHIS, and does not claim access at any named facility.

BetterHealth Africa connection is handled honestly: the Stool & Parasites panel's "Hidden bleeding: Stool RBCs" marker (`src/data/content.js`, ~line 317) is named plainly as one real, relevant data point, explicitly not equated with a certified FIT/FOBT colorectal screening test or claimed to have diagnostic accuracy. BetterHealth Africa does not perform colonoscopy or any other endoscopic procedure, stated clearly in prose and in the FAQ, following the same honest service-boundary pattern used in `lassa-fever-symptoms-test.js`.

   "Colonoscopy uses a thin, flexible camera to examine the entire colon directly, and it can remove a precancerous polyp in the same procedure it is found in."
