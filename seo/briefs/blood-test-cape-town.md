1. **Where to get tested** (id: `where-to-get-tested`)
   "In Cape Town, a blood test can be done at a public hospital laboratory, a private pathology lab, or through a home blood draw service that sends a phlebotomist to you."

2. **Public hospital labs** (id: `public-hospital-labs`)
   "Public hospital laboratories in Cape Town tend to be the most affordable route for a doctor-ordered test, though a referral, a queue and a longer wait for results usually come with that saving."

3. **Private labs** (id: `private-labs`)
   "Private pathology labs in Cape Town usually turn results around faster and offer a wider test menu than public facilities, though you pay out of pocket unless a medical aid plan covers the claim."

4. **Home blood draw** (id: `home-blood-draw`)
   "A home blood draw sends a trained phlebotomist to your home or office in Cape Town, so you can get tested without a trip to a lab or time in a waiting room."

5. **What to expect** (id: `what-to-expect`)
   "Most routine blood tests in Cape Town need little preparation, though some, such as a fasting glucose or lipid profile, ask you to fast for several hours first."

6. **How to choose** (id: `how-to-choose`)
   "Choosing where to get tested in Cape Town comes down to three questions: how urgent the test is, what your budget and medical aid cover allow, and whether this is routine screening or something a doctor has specifically ordered."

7. **Why screening matters** (id: `why-screening-matters`)
   "Routine blood test screening matters in Cape Town because conditions such as hypertension, type 2 diabetes and HIV carry a significant, well documented burden across South Africa, and many of them cause no symptoms until damage has already been done."
