## Council Review Synthesis

The audience here is not unaware of health checks and is not careless about health. They are time-poor. The job is to remove friction, not to add worry.

Council diagnosis:
- Crossing the Chasm (Lead): Pick one concrete beachhead — busy urban professionals in Ghana — and sell the "whole product," not just the test. The whole product is booking in minutes, collection at home or the office, secure MoMo or card payment, and doctor-reviewed results in plain language in the dashboard. Adoption follows when the entire path fits a working day, not when the test is cheaper or scarier.
- Positioning: Own a clear mental slot — "the health check that comes to you." Contrast is against the default in the reader's head: a half-day queue at a facility. Do not compete on fear; compete on fit.
- The Copywriter's Handbook: Lead with the real objection ("I don't have time"), then answer it step by step. Keep the structure benefit-first and the CTA single and obvious.
- Ogilvy on Advertising: Trust comes from specific, true detail — about 15 minutes, a certified phlebotomist, results reviewed by a doctor before they reach you, 48 to 72 hours. Only claim what the brief verifies.
- Chair synthesis: Make it aspirational-practical. The hero idea is "you can do this between meetings." Show the process is short and comes to them, keep the health framing light and non-diagnostic, and let the concrete, verified detail carry the credibility. No invented stats, no guilt.

## Core Message

For a lot of working professionals, the reason a health check keeps sliding down the list is time, not indifference. BetterHealth is built to fit a working schedule: you book in a few minutes, choose home or office sample collection anywhere in Ghana (the visit takes about 15 minutes), and pay with Mobile Money or card. A doctor reviews your results before they land in your dashboard, usually within 48 to 72 hours, with each health indicator explained in plain language. A health check is a useful starting point for looking after yourself — not a diagnosis on its own, but a practical step you can actually make room for.

## Clinical Guardrail

Do not present a health check or any single test as a diagnosis by itself. A check is a starting point; results should be read with symptoms, history, medicines, and other tests where needed. Keep the framing about access and convenience, not urgency or fear.

Do not revive the retired fallback formulations about panic and clearer next steps, or about symptoms and diagnosis. The approved line "Don't panic. Seek clarity." is fine to use.

Preferred framing:
- "The reason you skip a check is often time, not care."
- "A health check is a starting point, not the whole story."
- "Booking, testing, and results — around your workday, not instead of it."
- "Doctor-reviewed results, explained in plain language."
- "One result outside the reference range needs context before conclusions."

Operational claims are limited to the brief's verified facts: home or office collection anywhere in Ghana, about 15 minutes, MoMo or card payment, doctor-reviewed results in the dashboard within 48 to 72 hours. Do not add prices, turnaround guarantees, or specifics beyond these.

## Design Notes

- Primary visual mode: Real-Life Human Story. A busy professional in a realistic Ghana office or home setting — natural light, everyday workwear, a laptop and phone, not luxury stock or staged corporate gloss. Photorealistic Ghana-market realism.
- Alternative for the static/value-prop version: Strict Brand Image with the BetterHealth cream/sage/red palette and clean typographic step cards. Use this if a human scene can't be produced to a realistic standard.
- Hero (Slide 1 / Reel Shot 1): show the tension in under five seconds — a full calendar and a person who clearly cares but is out of time. Let the visual say "busy," so the copy can say "it fits."
- Generate backgrounds without text; add all typography, logo, step labels, CTA, and disclaimer as a composed overlay. Keep key copy and CTA inside platform safe zones.
- Carousel: hero slide is the emotional hook; step slides (3-5) get calmer and more structured — one idea per card. Final slide is positioning + CTA only.
- Do not show real result values, real dashboards with patient data, or invented numbers. Any dashboard mock is generic and plain-language, no specific readings.
- Footer disclaimer on educational/value-prop assets: `General education only. For severe or urgent symptoms, seek medical care.`
- Keep the collection scene respectful and clinical: a certified phlebotomist, gloves, calm and quick — reassuring, not clinical-scary.

## Hypothesis

Naming the real barrier — time, not care — will resonate more with busy professionals than a generic "get checked" message, because it removes guilt and reframes the decision as logistics. Pairing that reframe with a concrete, verified low-friction path (book in minutes, collection where you are, ~15 minutes, doctor-reviewed results in 48-72 hours) will drive more booking clicks than an aspirational message without a clear, fittable process.
