## Council Review Synthesis

This is a warm, relational post about people who love each other looking out for each other's health. It is not a symptom list and not a scare. The job is to make one small idea impossible to forget and easy to pass on.

Council diagnosis:
- Made to Stick: keep it simple, concrete, and emotional. One sticky idea: the people who remind you about everything else can remind you about your health too. Give the reader an exact, sayable line they can text to a parent, sibling, or partner.
- The Copywriter's Handbook: lead with the feeling, then make the next step effortless. The action is not "book a test today," it is "start the conversation." Lower the bar so sharing feels natural.
- Influence: this works through liking and care between family members, not fear or authority. Frame checking as something you do for each other, not something you are warned into. Keep the ask honest and gentle.
- Ogilvy on Advertising: earn trust by being respectful and unhurried. No pressure, no guilt, no invented statistics. The brand is the calm friend who helps when the family is ready, not the expert lecturing them.
- Chair synthesis: build the whole kit around one shareable sentence about care traveling between family members. Keep it emotionally resonant, keep every health idea hedged and general, and make the primary action "share this with someone you love," with booking as a soft, optional next step.

## Core Message

The people we love remind us about a lot of things, birthdays, appointments, taking it easy. Health can be part of that same care. A simple family conversation about getting checked, at whatever pace suits you, can make it easier for everyone to take a next step when they are ready. This is not about worrying together; it is about looking out for each other. A regular check can be a useful place to begin, and BetterHealth can help when your family decides the time is right.

## Clinical Guardrail

This is a relational, encouragement post, not a medical one. Do not list symptoms, do not imply anyone is sick, and do not suggest that a check confirms or rules out any condition. A health check is a useful starting point, not the whole story, and results always need context from a clinician.

Do not invent prevalence numbers, testimonials, or "families who did this" results. Use qualitative framing such as "many families" and "a common."

Do not revive the retired fallback formulations about panic and clearer next steps, or about symptoms and diagnosis. The short approved line "Don't panic. Seek clarity." is fine.

Preferred framing:
- "Care already travels between you. Health can too."
- "You remind each other about everything else. This can be part of it."
- "A check can be a useful place to begin."
- "At whatever pace suits your family."
- "Don't panic. Seek clarity."

## Design Notes

- Primary visual mode: Real-Life Human Story, with photorealistic Ghana-market realism. A warm, everyday family moment, an adult child and an older parent, siblings, or a couple, sharing food, a phone, or a quiet moment at home. Natural light, real homes, real clothes; not luxury stock or illustration.
- Alternative visual mode: Strict Brand Image, if a person-free asset is preferred. Clean BetterHealth cream canvas with sage and red accents and the hero line set large, no medical imagery.
- Generate the background image without text, then compose the headline, logo, CTA, and footer as an overlay in BetterHealth Media.
- Keep the emotional beat first: the image should communicate care between family members before the copy is read.
- Keep text inside platform safe zones; the hero line and CTA must stay readable at thumbnail size.
- Do not use symptom imagery, hospital settings, distressed faces, or anything that reads as fear. This is a warm, hopeful, unhurried post.
- Footer disclaimer on the static educational asset: `General education only. For severe or urgent symptoms, seek medical care.`

## Success Metrics

- Shares and sends to family/friends (primary metric for this post)
- Saves on Instagram
- Story poll and quiz responses
- Comments tagging family members
- DMs and profile visits
- Any downstream link-in-bio or book-test clicks (soft, secondary)

## Hypothesis

Framing the check as care that already travels between family members, and giving people one exact sentence to send, will drive more shares than a standard "book a test" prompt. Because the ask is emotional and low-pressure ("no rush, just together"), people will feel comfortable passing it to someone they love, which is the behaviour this post is built to create.
