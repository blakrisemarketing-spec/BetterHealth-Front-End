This post should make a confusing pair of tests feel simple without turning into a diabetes textbook.

Council diagnosis:
- The Copywriter's Handbook: use a direct comparison structure. Two columns, two jobs, one takeaway.
- Tested Advertising Methods: the strongest hook is not the test names. It is the contrast: "snapshot vs longer view."
- Ogilvy on Advertising: trust comes from explaining limitations. HbA1c is useful, but not perfect for everyone.
- Scientific Advertising: the save-worthy value is practical: which test needs fasting, what each test can help review, and when more than one test may be used.
- Made to Stick: the memorable line is: "One shows today. One shows the trend."
- Influence: invite sharing because many people hear "sugar test" without knowing which sugar test.
- Chair synthesis: make the carousel feel like a translation card. Do not diagnose, do not overwhelm with ranges, and do not imply one test is better in every situation.

## Core Message

Fasting blood sugar and HbA1c both help review blood sugar, but they do not show the same thing. A fasting blood sugar test measures your blood sugar at one point in time after fasting. HbA1c gives a longer view of average blood sugar over the past two to three months. Both can be useful, and the right one depends on the health question being asked.

## Clinical Guardrail

Do not present either test as a diagnosis by itself in social copy. Results should be read with symptoms, health history, medicines, risk factors, and other tests where needed.

Avoid saying one test is always better than the other. HbA1c can be affected by factors such as severe anaemia, some blood disorders including sickle cell-related haemoglobin variants, kidney failure, liver disease, pregnancy, blood loss/transfusion, and some medicines. Fasting blood sugar depends on correct fasting and captures only that moment.

Do not reuse the two retired Day 1 guardrail lines already documented in the pipeline rules.

Preferred framing:
- "One shows today. One shows the trend."
- "Fasting blood sugar is a snapshot."
- "HbA1c is the longer view."
- "They are not rivals. They answer different questions."
- "Your result needs context before conclusions."
