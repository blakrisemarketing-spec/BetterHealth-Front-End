## Council Review Synthesis

This post meets people at 1am, mid-scroll, half-frightened by a search result. The job is to name that spiral kindly, then offer a better move.

Council diagnosis:
- Tested Advertising Methods (lead): open on the exact felt moment — the midnight search — because a specific, recognisable scene stops the scroll faster than an abstract claim. The hook has to be the symptom-Googling spiral, not "get tested."
- Breakthrough Advertising: the audience is problem-aware and already anxious. Do not manufacture fear; it is already there. The desire is relief from uncertainty, so the mechanism to sell is "your own numbers, read with context" versus "everyone's worst-case stories."
- Made to Stick: anchor the whole idea on one repeatable, concrete contrast line: "Google can describe a symptom. It cannot measure your blood." Simple, unexpected, and true.
- Influence: reduce friction and preserve trust. Do not mock people for searching — searching is reasonable. Offer authority gently (doctor-reviewed results) and an easy next action.
- Chair synthesis: this is a redirect post, not a scare post. Validate the search, then move the reader from noise about other people's bodies to information about their own. Keep the tone empathetic; the enemy is uncertainty, not Google and not doctors.

## Core Message

Searching your symptoms online is a normal thing to do — it can be a useful place to begin, and it can help you ask better questions. But a search engine is reading the whole internet, not your body. It cannot measure your blood, your sugar, or your blood pressure. When worry keeps you up, the calmer next step is your own numbers, read with your symptoms, history, and medicines by someone who can put them in context. Don't panic. Seek clarity.

## Clinical Guardrail

Do not say a single test will tell you what is wrong or "rule out" anything. A test can give useful clues that need context; it is a starting point, not the whole story. Do not shame people for searching online, and do not crudely criticise doctors or search engines — the point is only that Google cannot measure your body. Use "can help", "may help", "useful clues", "needs context".

Do not revive the retired fallback formulations about panic and clearer next steps, or about symptoms and diagnosis. Keep the approved line "Don't panic. Seek clarity."

If symptoms are severe, sudden, or worsening — chest pain, trouble breathing, fainting, confusion, severe weakness, one-sided weakness, a very stiff neck with fever, heavy bleeding, or thoughts of self-harm — do not wait and do not search; seek medical care urgently.

Preferred framing:
- "Google can describe a symptom. It cannot measure your blood."
- "Search can give you fear and noise. It cannot give you your numbers."
- "A test read with context can. A search bar cannot."
- "Your body needs measuring, not just Googling."
- "Don't panic. Seek clarity."

## Design Notes

- Primary format is a Reel, but build a strong static cover frame that works on its own.
- Cover frame visual mode: Real-Life Human Story + Strict Brand Image. Hero shot is a Ghanaian person in a dark bedroom, face lit only by a phone, mid-scroll — instantly recognisable as the midnight-search moment.
- Cover hook (largest text): "Stop using Google as your lab result." Keep it as the first visible line.
- Secondary line on cover or second frame: "Google can describe a symptom. It cannot measure your blood." Give this line its own clean, calm frame in the reel — it is the save/share moment.
- Ghana-market realism: lived-in room details (wrapper on bed, charger, water cup, standing fan). Warm and ordinary, not luxury-stock, not flat illustration.
- Use light as the story: cold blue phone glow for the worry beats, warm morning light and cream BetterHealth cards for the clarity beats.
- Do not make the person look dangerously ill or helpless — they are worried, not in crisis. Avoid anything that mocks the person for searching.
- Keep the "Book a health test" CTA large and readable on mobile, inside platform safe zones.
- Footer disclaimer on the final frame and any educational static asset: `General education only. For severe or urgent symptoms, seek medical care.`

## Success Metrics

- Reel watch-through and replay rate
- Saves and shares (especially the "Google can describe a symptom" line frame)
- Comments admitting to the midnight-Googling spiral
- Story poll and quiz responses
- Profile visits and link-in-bio / book-test clicks

## Hypothesis

Opening on the universally recognised midnight symptom-search scene will out-perform a test-led hook, because the audience feels seen before they are asked to do anything. The concrete contrast line ("Google can describe a symptom. It cannot measure your blood.") gives a repeatable, share-worthy idea, and redirecting — rather than shaming — the search behaviour will drive book-test clicks without triggering defensiveness.
