## Council Review Synthesis

This is a trust post, not a test-education post. The reader has seen the ads and half-decided to book, but a quiet worry is holding them back: "What actually happens after I tap the button? Who shows up? What do I do with the results?" The job is to remove that uncertainty step by step.

Council diagnosis:
- Ogilvy on Advertising (Lead): trust is built from specific, verifiable detail, not adjectives. Name the real parts of the process — a certified phlebotomist, a confirmation SMS with the collector's name, a doctor reviewing results, a dashboard in plain English. Specifics are what make a promise believable. Do not oversell; let the handled process speak for itself.
- The Copywriter's Handbook: give the carousel a clean, numbered spine. Four steps, one idea per slide, in the order the reader will actually experience them. End by answering the unspoken objection ("Will I understand it?") before the CTA.
- Influence: this leans on authority and commitment/consistency. Authority comes from the certified collector, the doctor review, and the GDPC data-protection certification — stated plainly, never inflated. The small first commitment (booking) is made to feel safe because every following step is already taken care of.
- Scientific Advertising: make the next step measurable and concrete. The reader should finish knowing exactly what booking sets in motion and roughly when results arrive (48 to 72 hours), so the CTA is a low-risk, well-understood action.
- Chair synthesis: structure as a calm walk-through — hook, then the four real steps in order, then a reassurance slide on privacy and doctor review, then the CTA. Use only verified process facts. The emotional promise is simple: the process is handled, and a person and a doctor stand between you and a confusing result. Say what happens, in order, and let the specificity earn the trust.

## Core Message

Booking a test with BetterHealth starts a process that is handled for you, step by step. You choose your test and pay securely, get tested in about 15 minutes at a partner lab or through home collection anywhere in Ghana, and then a doctor reviews your results before they land in your dashboard within 48 to 72 hours, with each health indicator explained in plain language. You are not left alone with confusing numbers, and you can use the result to choose your next step.

## Clinical Guardrail

This is a process and trust post, so the discipline is factual accuracy, not clinical interpretation. State only the verified BetterHealth process facts. Do not invent turnaround times, prices, clinician names, sample-handling steps, or any step beyond the four-step flow and the documented trust facts.

Do not claim a test diagnoses, proves, or reveals a condition. A doctor reviewing a result before it reaches the dashboard is a safety and clarity step, not a diagnosis or a treatment plan. For anything concerning, the reader should discuss results with a healthcare provider.

Do not revive the retired fallback formulations about panic and clearer next steps, or about symptoms and diagnosis. The approved line "Don't panic. Seek clarity." is fine to use.

Preferred framing:
- "The process is handled, step by step."
- "A doctor reviews your results before they reach your dashboard."
- "You are not left alone with confusing numbers."
- "Each health indicator is explained in plain language."
- "Use your results to choose the right next step."
- "Don't panic. Seek clarity."

## Design Notes

- Primary visual mode: Explainer / Educational Carousel.
- Keep the calm beige canvas and sage accent from the existing kits; add a subtle numbered-step motif (1 to 4) so the sequence reads at a glance.
- Slide 1 (hero) should be scroll-stopping but calm: a phone with "Book a test" and the question "What happens next?" Get calmer and more orderly after the hero.
- Use one clear idea per slide, in process order. Slides 2, 3, 5, 7 carry the four numbered steps; slide 4 (home collection detail) and slide 6 (reassurance) and slide 8 (privacy) support them.
- For any human scene (collector arriving, someone reading results on a phone), use photorealistic Ghana-market realism, not luxury stock or illustration. A professional, friendly certified phlebotomist with a proper collection kit; an ordinary Ghanaian home or a real partner-lab setting.
- Represent the dashboard as a clean, readable phone screen with plainly labelled indicators. Do not put invented numbers, prices, reference ranges, or a real person's data on the mock screen; use neutral placeholder labels.
- Keep key copy and the CTA inside platform safe zones. Logo, page indicator, and disclaimer readable on every educational slide.
- Footer disclaimer on educational slides: `General education only. For severe or urgent symptoms, seek medical care.`
