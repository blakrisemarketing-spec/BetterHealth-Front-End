## Council Review Synthesis

This is the launch-sprint closer. The job is not to sell harder; it is to remove the quiet doubts that stop a ready person from booking. An FAQ works only if every answer is true and plainly stated.

Council diagnosis:
- Influence (Lead): trust is built by answering the objection before it is raised. The order matters, so lead with the biggest worry, privacy, then walk down the funnel: sample, results, concerning results, using results elsewhere. Authority here is earned by naming the real safeguards (GDPC certification, doctor review), not by sounding confident.
- Ogilvy on Advertising: credibility comes from specifics. "Certified by the Ghana Data Protection Commission under the Data Protection Act, 2012" reads as real because it is checkable. Keep every specific accurate and resist the urge to add impressive-sounding detail we cannot support.
- The Copywriter's Handbook: use a clean question-and-answer structure. One question per slide, plain answer, no jargon before plain English. The reader should be able to skim the questions alone and feel reassured.
- Scientific Advertising: the measurable next step is a booking or a DM. Give the reader an easy, low-pressure way to ask the one question still on their mind, so the FAQ converts curiosity into contact.
- Chair synthesis: build a reassuring FAQ, five real questions plus a hero and a CTA. Answer each from the verified facts only. The tone is "here is exactly how it works," never "trust us." Where health judgement is involved, point people to a healthcare provider rather than implying the platform decides for them.

## Core Message

Before you book a health test, it is fair to ask what happens to your data, your sample, and your results. Here are plain answers. Your information is protected, your sample is tracked so results connect back to the right booking, and a doctor reviews your results before they reach you, so you are not left alone with confusing numbers. Booking is direct, no referral needed, and you can share a clear report with your own doctor.

## Clinical Guardrail

This is a trust-and-process post, not a medical claim post. Keep the health boundary clear: the platform can flag a result that falls outside the normal range and notify you, but interpreting what a concerning result means is a conversation for a healthcare provider, not the platform.

Do not state any privacy or security specifics beyond the verified facts: GDPC certification under the Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843); data encrypted at rest and in transit; only you and providers you authorize can access your results; data is never sold. Do not invent retention periods, server locations, breach history, staff numbers, or certifications we do not have.

Do not revive the retired fallback formulations about panic and clearer next steps, or about symptoms and diagnosis. Keep the approved line "Don't panic. Seek clarity." — that one is preferred, not retired.

Preferred framing:
- "Only you and the providers you authorize can see your results."
- "A doctor reviews your results before they reach your dashboard."
- "Most results are ready within 48 to 72 hours."
- "You can download a report to use with your own doctor."
- "Discuss concerning results with a healthcare provider."
- "Don't panic. Seek clarity."

## Design Notes

- Primary visual mode: Explainer / Educational Carousel, with a Strict Brand Image treatment on the hero and CTA slides so the trust message reads as calm and official.
- Keep the BetterHealth cream canvas with sage and a restrained red accent. Each FAQ slide uses the same layout: the question as a clear headline in quote form, the plain answer below.
- Use simple, purposeful icons, one per slide: a lock (privacy), a labelled sample tube (sample), a dashboard with a clock (results), a gentle alert (concerning results), a document with a share arrow (PDF report). Avoid clinical stock photography and anything that looks like a hospital textbook.
- If a human scene is used on the hero, use photorealistic Ghana-market realism: an ordinary person with a phone, calm and unposed, not luxury stock.
- Get the questions calmer after the hero. The CTA slide should feel like an open door, not a hard sell.
- Keep all text and the CTA inside platform safe zones. Do not let icons cover the headline.
- Footer disclaimer on every educational slide: `General education only. For severe or urgent symptoms, seek medical care.`
