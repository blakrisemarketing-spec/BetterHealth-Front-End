## Council Review Synthesis

This post has to motivate earlier action without tipping into fear. The reader is often busy, feels fine, and quietly assumes "no symptoms" means "no problem." The job is to gently challenge that assumption with honesty, not alarm.

Council diagnosis:
- Influence (lead): the persuasion here rests on trusted authority and honest framing, not pressure. People act earlier when a credible source tells the truth plainly, including the limits. Say clearly that checking earlier gives useful information and a chance to act sooner, and say just as clearly that it does not catch everything. Honesty about the limit is what earns the trust that moves someone to book.
- Breakthrough Advertising: the reader is problem-aware but not yet action-aware. The desire to protect their future is already there; the belief gap is "checking only matters once I feel something." The mechanism to install is simple and true: some serious conditions can be quiet early, so a check can surface useful clues before you would notice anything.
- Ogilvy on Advertising: credibility comes from specifics handled respectfully. Name a few conditions that can be silent early (raised blood pressure, high blood sugar, some kidney changes) without inventing numbers or scaring anyone. Trust is built by what you refuse to overclaim.
- Made to Stick: the sticky, repeatable idea is "quiet does not always mean fine." Concrete, unexpected, and easy to pass on.
- Chair synthesis: lead with the honest tension ("feeling fine is good news, but it is not the whole story"), install the mechanism (some conditions can be silent early), then make the next step small and non-scary: learn what is worth checking. Keep every line hedged, invent no statistics, and never imply a single check confirms or rules out disease.

## Core Message

Feeling fine is good news, but it is not the whole story. Some serious conditions, such as raised blood pressure, high blood sugar, and certain kidney changes, can be quiet in their early stages and often have no clear symptoms at first. Checking a few key markers before symptoms appear can give useful clues sooner and a chance to act earlier. It is a starting point, not a guarantee: preventive testing does not catch everything, and results should be read with your symptoms, history, medicines, and other tests where needed.

## Clinical Guardrail

What not to claim:
- Do not say a preventive check "rules out" disease, "proves" you are healthy, or "diagnoses" anything on its own.
- Do not imply that a normal result today means nothing can be wrong or that no future checks are needed.
- Do not invent prevalence numbers, percentages, risk figures, or "X in Y" claims. Use qualitative framing only ("often has no early symptoms", "can be silent", "many people").
- Do not use fear-heavy or catastrophising language to drive booking.
- Do not present a symptom list as a self-diagnosis tool.

Do not revive the retired fallback formulations about panic and clearer next steps, or about symptoms and diagnosis. The approved short line "Don't panic. Seek clarity." is fine to use.

Preferred framing:
- "Feeling fine is good news, but it is not the whole story."
- "Some conditions can be quiet early."
- "Checking earlier can give useful clues sooner, and a chance to act earlier."
- "A starting point, not a guarantee."
- "It does not catch everything; results need context."
- "Read with your symptoms, history, medicines, and other tests where needed."
- "Don't panic. Seek clarity."

Urgent-symptom safety note (include on educational assets): For severe or urgent symptoms, do not wait for a routine check; seek medical care.

## LinkedIn Post

Most of us treat "I feel fine" as a full health report.

It is good news. But it is not the whole story.

Some of the conditions that matter most, like raised blood pressure, high blood sugar, and certain kidney changes, can be quiet in their early stages. They often have no clear symptoms at first. You can feel completely well while something is quietly worth a closer look.

That is the honest case for checking a few key markers before symptoms appear.

Not because a check is a crystal ball. It is not. Preventive testing does not catch everything, and no single result confirms or rules out disease on its own.

But checking earlier can do two useful things:

1. It can surface useful clues sooner, while there is more time to act.
2. It can give you and a clinician a clearer starting point for the next conversation.

A few markers often worth knowing, and worth discussing with a clinician:

- Blood pressure
- Blood sugar
- Cholesterol / lipids
- Kidney function

Each of these is a starting point, not the whole story. A result outside the reference range needs context: your symptoms, health history, medicines, and other tests where needed.

The useful shift is small. It is moving from "I will check when something feels wrong" to "I will check a few key markers while I feel well, so I have better information either way."

## Design Notes

- Primary visual mode: Strict Brand Image for the single static / hero card; Explainer / Educational Carousel if produced as the multi-card "what to check" set.
- Keep the calm BetterHealth palette (cream/beige canvas, sage accent, restrained red only for emphasis). This is a reassurance-led post, so avoid alarm colours, warning icons, or clinical stock imagery.
- If a human scene is used, use photorealistic Ghana-market realism: an ordinary person looking well and unbothered during a normal day, not a worried patient. The visual should say "you can feel fine and still benefit from a check," not "be afraid."
- Hero must explain the idea in under five seconds: "Feeling fine? That is good news, but not the whole story."
- Keep the four marker labels (blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol/lipids, kidney function) as plain, evenly weighted cards; do not add ranges, thresholds, or numbers.
- The honest-limit card ("a starting point, not a guarantee") is the trust moment; give it clear visual space rather than burying it.
- Because the primary format is LinkedIn + Facebook, prioritise a clean single hero image plus strong text; the carousel/stories are the repurpose layer.
- Footer disclaimer on every educational asset: General education only. For severe or urgent symptoms, seek medical care.
- Keep key copy and CTA inside platform safe zones.

## Hypothesis

Leading with the honest tension ("feeling fine is good news, but not the whole story") and openly naming the limit of preventive testing will build more trust, and drive more "learn what to check" clicks, than a fear-based "catch it early or else" angle. Honesty about what a check cannot do should make the invitation to check feel safe rather than alarming.
