# Wellness Consultation — Consultant Script

**For:** BetterHealth Wellness Consultants
**Call length:** 15–20 minutes, Google Meet or phone call
**Companion docs:** `WELLNESS_PLAN_TEMPLATE.md` · `../WELLNESS_CONSULTATION_CAMPAIGN_PLAN.md`

---

## Before you read anything else: what this call is

The person on the other end did not book a call to buy a lab test. They booked
because something is on their mind and nobody has ever sat down and helped them
make sense of it.

Your job is to help them **name what they're actually worried about** and leave
them with a written plan. A test is usually part of that plan. It is not the point
of the call, and if you run this like a sales call it will convert worse, not better.

**The honest frame:** we make money when someone books a test. Say so early
(see the opening). People forgive a business that's upfront. They do not forgive
a "free consultation" that turns out to be a pitch at minute 22.

---

## Scope — the hard lines

| You **may** | You **may not** |
|---|---|
| Explain what a test measures, in plain English | Diagnose anything, or say someone "has" a condition |
| Help someone set health goals | Interpret a result — that's the doctor consult, after the test |
| Recommend a panel and explain why | Advise on starting, stopping, or changing any medicine |
| Explain how testing and results review work | Contradict something the person's own doctor has told them |
| Say "that needs a doctor's eyes" | Give a timeline or prognosis |

If you find yourself about to say *"that sounds like…"* — stop. The correct sentence
is **"that's worth checking properly, and it needs a doctor to read it in context."**

### Red flags — end the health discussion and direct to care, today

Chest pain or pressure · breathlessness at rest · fainting or blackouts · sudden
weakness, numbness, facial droop or trouble speaking · severe or sudden headache ·
coughing or vomiting blood · blood in stool · severe abdominal pain · fever with
confusion or a stiff neck · heavy unexplained weight loss · any pregnancy concern ·
any mention of self-harm.

> "I'm going to stop us there, because what you've described is beyond what I can
> help with on a call like this — and it deserves to be looked at properly today,
> not next week. Please go to a hospital or call your doctor. I'll follow up
> tomorrow to check you're okay."

Log it in the bookings sheet, do not recommend a test, do not continue the plan.
Follow up the next day as promised.

## The 20 minutes

The call is booked as a 20-minute slot and should land at 15–20. Writing the plan
happens *after* the call, not in it — do not eat into the next booking.

### 0–2 min · Open and frame

> "Hi [name], thanks for making the time. I'm [name], a Wellness Consultant with
> BetterHealth.
>
> Here's how the next twenty minutes work. I'm going to ask you some questions about
> your health and what's on your mind. Then we'll put together a wellness plan —
> a real one, written down, that I'll send you on WhatsApp within a day. It'll have
> a few things to work on, and it'll usually include some blood work, because
> that's how we find out where you're actually starting from rather than guessing.
>
> The testing is the part we charge for. Everything else — this call, the plan,
> the follow-up — is free. I'll tell you exactly what I think you should check and
> why, and you can decide about it in your own time. No pressure from me either way.
>
> One thing before we start: I'm not a doctor, so I won't be diagnosing anything
> today. If something needs a doctor, I'll say so. Sound good?"

**Why the money is mentioned in minute two:** it removes the thing they're bracing
for. Every minute they spend wondering "when's the pitch coming" is a minute they
aren't telling you the truth.

---

### 2–9 min · Discovery

**Questions 1–7 are the call. Questions 8–10 are for when the person is talkative
and you have room** — do not rush the first seven to fit the last three in.

Ask, then be quiet. The single most common mistake is filling the silence.
Take notes verbatim where you can — their own words go into the plan, and seeing
their own words reflected back is what makes the plan feel like theirs.

1. **"What made you book this call?"**
   Ask it first, ask it plainly, and don't help them answer. This is the real driver.
2. **"How would you describe your health right now, in your own words?"**
3. **"When you think about your health, what worries you most?"**
   This is the most important question on the page. Wait for it.
4. **"Has anything changed in the last six months or so — energy, weight, sleep, mood?"**
5. **"What runs in your family? Parents, brothers and sisters — anything like
   diabetes, high blood pressure, stroke, heart trouble, cancer?"**
   Family history is the strongest honest lever there is. It is a fact about their
   life, not a scare tactic.
6. **"When did you last have blood work done? Do you remember what it showed?"**
7. **"Are you taking any medicines or supplements regularly?"**
8. **"Walk me through a normal day — when do you eat, what does moving around look like?"**
   No judgement in your voice. You are gathering, not grading.
9. **"If we could sort out one thing about your health in the next three months,
   what would you pick?"**
10. **"What's stopped you from doing something about this before?"**
    Surfaces the real objection while there's still time to address it — usually
    cost, time, or fear of what they'll find.

---

### 9–12 min · Reflect back

This is where felt need is created, and it is created by **accurate listening,
not by warnings.**

> "Let me say back what I heard, and tell me where I've got it wrong.
>
> You're [age], you feel mostly fine, but [their words about what changed].
> Your [family member] had [condition] at [age], and that's sat at the back of your
> mind for a while. You've never had [X] checked. And the thing you'd most like to
> sort out is [their answer to Q9].
>
> Have I got that right?"

Then the pivot — one sentence, no drama:

> "So here's the thing. Everything you've told me is about the future — what might
> happen, what happened to your dad. Right now none of us knows where you actually
> stand, including you. That's the gap. A plan starts by closing it."

**Do not** predict, warn, or use a percentage. Do not say what they might have.
The tension is already in the room — they brought it. Naming it clearly is enough.

---

### 12–16 min · Build the plan out loud

Say it in the shape of the document they'll receive, so it arrives familiar.

> "Your plan is going to have three parts.
>
> **Where you are now.** That's the blood work — [name the panel] — and it covers
> [2–3 things in their own language, e.g. 'your sugar, your cholesterol, and how
> your kidneys are coping'].
>
> **Three things to work on.** Based on what you've told me: [goal 1], [goal 2],
> [goal 3]. These come from what you already said you wanted, not from a template.
>
> **A date to check back.** [Timeframe], so we can see whether the numbers moved."

At least one goal must be something they can start **this week without spending
anything**. A plan whose every step costs money reads as a shopping list.

---

### 16–18 min · The recommendation

> "For what you've described, I'd start with the **[panel name]** — that's
> **[GHS X]**. It covers [the things they told you they were worried about].
>
> I've picked it because you mentioned [their reason], not because it's the biggest
> one we do. There's a bigger one and there are smaller ones — happy to walk through
> either if you'd rather."

Then stop talking. Let them respond.

If they're ready: book it on the call, with them, on the phone. Do not send a link
and hope. Confirm collection or lab visit, and confirm the results timeline —
48 to 72 hours, then a doctor consultation to go through what it means.

If they're not ready: **do not push.** Say the plan comes either way and that the
recommendation will be in it, priced, so they can decide in their own time.
Roughly a third of people book later; nobody books later after being squeezed.

---

### 18–20 min · Close

> "Two things from me. The plan lands on your WhatsApp within a day — it'll have
> your goals, what I've recommended, and what it costs.
>
> Your reference is **[REF CODE]** — quote it whenever you're ready and we'll pick
> up right where we left off.
>
> And if anything comes up before then, message me directly on this number."

---

## Panel quick-reference

Recommend the **narrowest panel that covers what they actually told you.** A person
who books the GHS 350 panel and returns is worth more than one who is talked into
GHS 1,100 once and never comes back.

| If the conversation centred on… | Start with | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Blood sugar, diabetes in the family, thirst or weight change | Dialics — Blood Sugar Check | GHS 350 |
| Blood pressure, cholesterol, stroke or heart in the family | Cardion — Heart Health Check | GHS 475 |
| "I just want to know I'm okay", no specific worry | Shield — Wellness Check | GHS 497 |
| Several things at once — sugar *and* heart *and* organs | Metabolix — Core Health Check | GHS 697 |
| Wants the full picture, one sitting | Panorama — Complete Health Check | GHS 1,100 |
| Men's health specifically | Alpha — Men's Health Check | GHS 995 |
| Women's health specifically | Empress — Women's Health Check | GHS 995 |
| Trying to conceive | Spark — Fertility Test | GHS 1,500 |
| Sexual health, wants discretion | Privara — Private STI Check | GHS 897 |

### Single tests — check the arithmetic before you offer one

Live prices (verified against the catalogue 2026-08-09 — **do not quote from memory,
these moved a lot**):

Fasting Blood Sugar **45** · Malaria RDT **40** · Uric Acid **75** · Urine RE **85** ·
HIV **100** · Hepatitis B **100** · Full Blood Count **150** · HbA1c **170** ·
Liver **190** · Lipid/Cholesterol **195** · Kidney **195** · Thyroid **435**

Singles are only genuinely cheaper at the bottom of that list. Two mid-priced singles
already cost more than Dialics (GHS 350, three tests), so **once someone needs more than
one thing, the panel is usually both cheaper and more useful.** Do the sum on the call
rather than reaching for "single test" as the automatic budget answer.

> "Let's start with the one that answers your biggest question. We can always
> add to it."

## Objections

**"Is it really free? What's the catch?"**
> "No catch. The call and the plan are free. We charge for the lab work, and only
> if you decide to do it. Plenty of people take the plan and go away and think about
> it — that's completely fine."

**"Do I have to do the test?"**
> "Not at all. The plan is yours either way. The test is how we stop guessing, but
> it's your call and it'll still be there next month."

**"I feel fine, so why test?"**
> "That's actually the best time to check — you get a baseline while things are
> good, so if anything shifts later you've got something to compare against.
> Plenty of the things we look at give useful clues long before anyone feels
> anything at all."

**"It's expensive."**
> "I hear you. Let's not do the big one then. What's the single question you most
> want answered? There's usually a way to answer that one for [GHS 50–180], and
> the rest can wait."

**"I did a test last year."**
> "Good — that's more than most people. Do you still have it? If you send it over,
> the doctor can compare, and we may only need to recheck a couple of things rather
> than start over."

**"Let me talk to my husband/wife."**
> "Of course. I'll get the plan to you today so you've got something to look at
> together rather than trying to remember what I said."

**"Can't I just do this at the hospital?"**
> "You can, and if you have a doctor you trust, do. What we add is that we help you
> pick what to check, and a doctor sits down with you afterwards to go through what
> it actually means — which is the part people usually leave the hospital without."

---

## After the call — same day

1. Write the plan from your notes and send it on WhatsApp **within 24 hours.**
   Late plans convert badly; the feeling you created on the call has a shelf life.
2. Log in the bookings sheet: ref code, attended, plan sent, test booked Y/N,
   panel, value, and one line on what they actually wanted. That last field is how
   the ads team learns which angle to buy more of.
3. If no test was booked: nudge at day 3 and day 7, then stop. Two follow-ups, no more.

---

## Language rules

Preferred: "can help" · "may help" · "useful clues" · "often a useful place to
begin" · "needs context" · "read alongside your symptoms, history and medicines".

Never: "will reveal" · "proves" · "diagnoses" · "rules out everything" ·
"always the right place to start".

Lead with the plain idea, then the medical word — not the other way round.
Don't reach for fear. Don't imply BetterHealth knows everything.

*General education only. For severe or urgent symptoms, seek medical care.*
